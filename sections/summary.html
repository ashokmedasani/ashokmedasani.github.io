Summary

From predicting system failures to predicting outcomes in data

I started in banking infrastructure — production support on HP NonStop systems that underpinned card and transaction processing at Bank of America. My days were alerts: batch jobs running long, traffic and CPU degradation, failing over between East and West Coast data centers.

Two things pushed me toward analytics. The first was impatience with manual work — internal audit reporting was rebuilt by hand every week from Jira and ITSM exports, so I automated it: pick a date range, get the report with the charts already built. That saved 100+ operational hours a month and led to my role as change/QA subject matter expert, which I went on to lead with a team of 15.

The second was more interesting. Our alerts looked random, but they weren't — after a change went in, the alerts it caused often surfaced later, not immediately. I kept thinking: this is a pattern, so why are we reacting to it instead of predicting it? I didn't have the tools then. That question is what brought me to the M.S. in Analytics at Saint Louis University.

Since then I've built CareFinder, a Django healthcare application forecasting hospital cost and patient visits with Random Forest and XGBoost across ten integrated datasets; trained and deployed CNNs; shipped a RAG assistant benchmarking embedding strategies; and designed and deployed a full multi-surface platform on AWS. I'm now focused on AI/ML Engineer and Applied AI roles, and currently extending into symbolic regression research under a professor at Saint Louis University — alongside data analytics, data science, and analytics engineering, where I have the same depth.

🤖 AI / ML Engineering
Model development and deployment across three problem types: predictive/tabular (CareFinder, TitanForge — Random Forest, XGBoost, Gradient Boosting, SMOTE for class imbalance), computer vision (CNNs, ResNet transfer learning, deployed on Hugging Face), and GenAI (RAG, embeddings benchmarking, agentic workflows, Databricks GenAI Fundamentals accreditation). Currently researching symbolic regression under SLU faculty supervision.
